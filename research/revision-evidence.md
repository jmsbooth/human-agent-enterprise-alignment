# Revision evidence and audit

This is an internal, auditable revision artifact for the paper in paper/main.tex. It records the evidence boundary and the decisions used to implement the Cross-Layer Alignment revision specification. It is not a systematic review, meta-analysis, confidence assessment, or empirical validation report.

## A. Revision objective

The manuscript is now a structured interdisciplinary conceptual synthesis and perspective paper. Its core contribution is a bounded problem definition: cross-layer alignment among semantic foundation, actor and organizational topology, process representation, strategic intent and architecture, and governance constraints around delegated agent action.

The revision must not present:

- an alignment equation;
- a safe-delegation equation;
- a universal scalar alignment score;
- synthetic scores as research evidence; or
- the exploratory repository code as a paper contribution or validation model.

The manuscript must preserve:

- the mismatch-centered framing;
- distinct semantic, structural, process, and strategic dimensions;
- governance as a cross-cutting constraint plane;
- prescribed, observed, authorized, and effective topology;
- explicit, observed, inferable, and tacit knowledge distinctions;
- evidence categories with clear non-confidence semantics;
- the optional DTO evidence-substrate discussion; and
- an empirical research agenda without a predetermined equation.

## C. Novelty challenge matrix

| Objection or adjacent work | What it already does | What remains under-specified | Surviving distinction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Agentic BPM already combines agents, processes, goals, and guardrails | Agentic BPM makes process-aware agents, goals, normative frames, explanation, adaptation, and governance first-class | It does not consistently provide a reusable comparison protocol for semantic, topology, process, strategic, and governance relations across a bounded action | Alignment across representations, not combination of ingredients |
| DEMO and enterprise ontology already combine ontology, roles, and transactions | They connect enterprise meaning, actor roles, transactions, and process structure | They do not by themselves address dynamic human-agent teams, runtime authority, or evidence status across observed and authorized practice | Cross-layer diagnostic framing |
| Semantic BPM already connects ontology and process | Semantic BPM and BPMN ontology work link concepts to process models | Actor authority, team topology, strategic ownership, and runtime governance remain distinct questions | Semantic foundation as one dimension among several |
| Agent Miner and organizational mining recover agent topology from event data | Event logs can support discovery of agents, agent types, interactions, and modular models | Observed topology is not authorized topology, and discovery does not establish semantic, strategic, or normative agreement | Observed topology as evidence, not as a complete organizational model |
| Human-AI teaming models roles, leadership, interdependence, communication, and trust | HAT and HAT typology work operationalize team configurations and human outcomes | Team-level constructs are rarely connected to enterprise ontology, process conformance, architecture, and delegated authority | Actor topology as a boundary object, not a new team taxonomy |
| Enterprise architecture maps strategy, capabilities, organization, and systems | Zachman, TOGAF, and ArchiMate provide viewpoints and traceability | Runtime agent behavior, event-level conformance, dynamic delegation, and HAT constructs are not usually joined to those viewpoints | Strategic intent and architecture as a distinct layer |
| DTO research proposes organization twins | DTO work links organizational design, process, event data, constraints, and action engines | Existing DTOs do not consistently operationalize agent-specific authority and cross-layer evidence status | DTO as an optional substrate, not a required architecture |

## D. Construct-definition audit

| Construct | Working definition | Relevant representations and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-layer alignment | Evidence-supported correspondence or compatibility among representations relevant to a bounded delegated action | Versioned semantic, topology, process, strategic, and governance representations |
| Misalignment | Evidence-supported conflict, omitted required constraint, or incompatible authorization and enactment | Conflicting mappings, ownership, process, permission, or intent evidence |
| Unknown or insufficient evidence | Evidence is too incomplete, stale, ambiguous, or contradictory to determine agreement | Missing telemetry, unresolved identity, uncertain scope, or contested meaning |
| Semantic foundation | Machine-accessible meanings of entities, actions, states, constraints, commitments, and exceptions | Ontologies, knowledge graphs, schemas, taxonomies, semantic layers, mappings, provenance |
| Actor topology | Actor identity and type, role assignment, responsibility boundaries, authority, delegation, interaction, autonomy, interfaces, and organizational or software boundaries | Prescribed, observed, authorized, and effective topology; identity and event records |
| Process representation | Prescribed or intended work and its observed execution, variants, handoffs, decisions, and exceptions | BPMN, DEMO, process traces, tool traces, case records, escalation records |
| Strategic intent and architecture | Objectives, capabilities, priorities, risk appetite, ownership, dependencies, and the architectural context for action | Objective maps, capability maps, architecture viewpoints, service catalogs, risk records |
| Governance constraint plane | Controls determining permissible identity, permission, prohibition, obligation, delegation, approval, separation of duties, monitoring, attribution, audit, revocation, interruptibility, and autonomy | Policies, identity records, authorization decisions, approval records, audit and monitoring evidence |
| Tacit knowledge | Relevant knowledge enacted in practice but not fully represented in machine-accessible artifacts | Informal escalation, expert judgment, local adaptation, interviews, uninstrumented work |

Missing explicit representation is not automatically misalignment. A classification requires task scope, representation versions, evidence provenance, and contextual interpretation.

## E. Mismatch and proposition audit

The manuscript expands the mismatch catalog to include:

1. semantic and process;
2. structural and governance;
3. process and topology;
4. strategic intent and process;
5. semantic and governance;
6. observed and prescribed;
7. authorized and observed topology;
8. process and governance;
9. semantic and structural;
10. architecture and organizational ownership; and
11. strategic intent and granted autonomy.

Each entry records the compared representations, an example disagreement, possible evidence or consequence, and a caveat. The code catalog uses unknown as the default status because it contains no organization-specific evidence.

| Proposition | Evidence boundary after revision |
| --- | --- |
| P1: shared semantic representations can improve bounded task accuracy, domain consistency, constraint adherence, or traceability | Strong adjacent and emerging direct; depends on coverage, freshness, retrieval, entity resolution, and task design |
| P2: better-specified topology and bounded authority can improve responsibility clarity and expose or constrain inconsistent interactions | Emerging direct and strong adjacent; incident blast radius is a future outcome, not an established consequence |
| P3: explicit process representation and event evidence can expose exception, conformance, handoff, and attribution failures | Strong adjacent and emerging direct; inspectability does not automatically prevent failure |
| P4: joint consideration of the layers may provide value beyond any isolated dimension | Conceptual/speculative; central integration proposition and least empirically established; a null interaction is also informative |
| P5: explicit uncertainty and separate alignment, misalignment, and unknown labels may improve autonomy, escalation, and review decisions | Conceptual/emerging; requires inter-rater and decision-quality studies |

P4 is intentionally not a validated interaction model. No numerical relationship is preselected.

## G. Implementation acceptance audit

- paper/main.tex is a conceptual synthesis and perspective paper.
- The manuscript contains no equation environments or scalar alignment model.
- The manuscript explicitly separates alignment, misalignment, and unknown evidence.
- Actor topology remains prose-defined and distinguishes prescribed, observed, authorized, and effective forms.
- Semantic, structural, process, and strategic dimensions remain distinct.
- Governance is described as a cross-cutting constraint plane with the required control vocabulary and no numeric coverage claim.
- The mismatch catalog contains all eleven required mismatch relations.
- P2 is softened; P4 is marked central and least empirically established.
- DTO is optional and incomplete by design.
- experimental/mismatch_exploration.py contains no scoring or validation routine.
- README.md describes the paper and code without presenting synthetic research evidence.
- Tests validate the exploratory taxonomy and serialization only.
